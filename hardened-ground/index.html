“Today when you hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts
as Israel did when they rebelled.”

Hebrews 3:15, NLT

The Course

A good many of us were raised in the faith, or came to it later, and then settled into what we were told it meant. We learned the phrases. We learned which answers were expected. What we did not always do was sit down with the Bible, read it carefully, and let it question us back. Over time those inherited readings harden. A living insight becomes simply “the way it is.” The philosophers have a name for this. Edmund Husserl called it sedimentation (Sedimentierung). Peter Berger and Thomas Luckmann described how meanings settle until they feel like the natural order of things. You don’t need the technical term to know it when you see it. It happens in a Bible study when someone says, “I already studied this, and you will not change my mind.”

In this class we are going to try to loosen that ground.

Jesus’ teaching keeps moving. It upsets the status quo, and it will not be fenced in by our conclusions. The prophets were resisted in their own day and later shown to be true. The Spirit, Jesus says, “will guide you into all truth” (John 16:13, NLT), and he is still doing that guiding. Along the way we will learn to tell two kinds of time apart: chronos, the measured passing of hours in which understanding hardens, and kairos, the appointed moment that calls for a decision. The thread through every week is Hebrews 3:15. Today, when you hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts. The course ends where Jesus sends his disciples out: “As the Father has sent me, so I am sending you” (John 20:21, NLT).

Purpose and Big Idea

My hope is that this class opens our hearts. I want us to move from a faith we inherited to one we have examined, and from a passive, Sunday-only faith to one that goes out into the public square. Jesus taught us to pray, “May your Kingdom come soon. May your will be done on earth, as it is in heaven” (Matthew 6:10, NLT). If what we see around us is not on earth as it is in heaven, we are doing it wrong, and it needs disrupting.

Who It Is For

This class is for the whole church: long-time members, newer believers, and anyone willing to open Scripture and look again. No seminary vocabulary required. Come willing to let the Word interrupt you.

What We Hope You Will Take With You

If this class does its work, you will be better able to:

  1. Tell chronos from kairos, and notice how understanding can harden over ordinary time.
  2. Read familiar passages with a more open and careful eye, and stop settling for the platitudes we inherited.
  3. Recognize the biblical pattern of prophetic resistance and later vindication.
  4. Describe how Jesus and the Spirit disrupt settled religious habits.
  5. Practice an examined faith that moves from the Sunday gathering into the public square, so that God’s will might be done on earth as it is in heaven.
  6. Hear Hebrews 3:15 as a word spoken to us today, and keep a soft heart when it comes.

How Each Session Works

Every week follows the same simple shape, so you always know where you are:

  • Setting. We start with the week’s question.
  • Context. We notice what was happening when the text was first heard.
  • Scripture. We read the passage slowly, in the New Living Translation, and sit with it.
  • Reflection. We think together about what the text is doing, and how ground hardens around it.
  • For Discussion. We take up questions meant to get the room talking.

The Sessions

  • Chronos and Kairos. Measured time and the appointed moment.
  • How Ground Hardens. How a living insight settles into “the way it is.”
  • Certainty That Isn’t True. The difference between conviction and closure.
  • Prophets and Resistance. Resisted first, vindicated later.
  • Jesus the Great Disruptor. Upside-down teaching that will not leave us settled.
  • The Spirit Who Will Not Be Fenced In. The Spirit is still leading.
  • Running from the Call. Jonah, and what our refusal costs the people around us.
  • Recognizing the Visitation. Reading the moment we are in. The finished work launches the mission. We are sent.
