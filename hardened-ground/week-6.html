Week 6

The Spirit Who Will Not Be Fenced In

Takeaway. The Spirit’s guidance is ongoing. A conclusion we have already filed away may still be opened.

Opening question. Where might the Spirit be trying to lead you past a conclusion you had already closed?

Setting

We look at the Holy Spirit, who will not stay inside our settled conclusions.

Context

In John 3, Jesus tells Nicodemus that the Spirit is like the wind: you hear it, but you do not manage it. In John 16 he promises that the Spirit of truth will guide the disciples into all truth, and that work is still going on. Acts 10 shows that promise in motion. Peter is led to Cornelius, and a boundary he thought was settled is opened.

Scripture

Texts. John 3:8; John 16:13; Acts 10

“The wind blows wherever it wants. Just as you can hear the wind but can’t tell where it comes from or where it is going, so you can’t explain how people are born of the Spirit.”

John 3:8, NLT

“When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all truth. He will not speak on his own but will tell you what he has heard.”

John 16:13, NLT

Reflection

  1. Wind that blows where it wants. Stay with John 3:8 long enough for the room to feel it. The Spirit does not fit our systems. We can hear the wind, but we don’t steer it. Nicodemus is a serious teacher, and even he is being asked to start over.
  2. Guidance into all truth. John 16:13 promises that the leading will go on. The Wesleyan habit of testing tradition against Scripture, reason, and lived experience leaves the same opening: tradition is received, but it is not frozen. The Spirit may still teach the church what it thought it had already finished learning.
  3. Peter and Cornelius. Tell the story of Acts 10 with some care: the vision, the journey, the household, the Spirit falling on people Peter had not planned to include. Peter is no rebel. He is a faithful man, and his certainty gets interrupted. The Spirit opens a boundary the church had already filed away. Ask what it cost Peter to say yes, and what it would have cost him to refuse.
  4. Hardening around the Spirit himself. Arguments about the Holy Spirit can become some of the most sedimented arguments in a church, because they feel already decided. The line from week 2 returns here: “I already studied this, and you will not change my mind.” If the Spirit is wind, that sentence is a closed window. Ask the room to keep their convictions and stay teachable.

For Discussion

  1. What does Jesus’ image of the wind tell us about trying to manage the Spirit?
  2. If the Spirit “will guide you into all truth,” what does that imply about conclusions we have already reached?
  3. In Acts 10, what was Peter so sure of, and what did it take for that certainty to be opened?
  4. Where has the church, in history or in your own memory, had to admit that the Spirit was ahead of its settled boundaries?
  5. What would it look like to hold your convictions about the Spirit with a soft heart rather than a closed file?
  6. Where might the Spirit be trying to lead this group, or this church, past a conclusion we had already closed?
